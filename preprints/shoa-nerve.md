## Abstract

We present SHOA-Nerve — the fifth protocol in the SHOA medical line. Nerve tissue has limited regenerative capacity. Spinal cord injuries, peripheral neuropathies, and neurodegenerative diseases remain incurable. We propose that a Grape-impulse tuned to the resonance frequency of neuronal microtubules can activate quiescent Schwann cells, promote axonal regrowth, and restore synaptic connectivity. The protocol integrates Spiked Voting across quantum dot clusters embedded in a biocompatible nerve guide conduit, triggering localized Grape-impulses upon detection of axonal damage. We provide the theoretical framework, conduit architecture, and a phased experimental roadmap.

## 1. Introduction

Neurons communicate via electrical and chemical signals. Their structural integrity depends on microtubules — cylindrical polymers of tubulin that maintain axonal transport and cellular shape. Damage to these microtubules disrupts Ψ-coherence at the cellular level. SHOA-Nerve targets this disruption.

---

## 2. Mechanism

The Grape-impulse, tuned to the resonance frequency of tubulin dimers (~10^10 Hz, corresponding to their dipole oscillations), activates Schwann cells at the injury site. The impulse stimulates:

- Schwann cell proliferation and myelination.
- Axonal sprouting and directed regrowth toward the distal stump.
- Re-establishment of synaptic vesicle cycling.
- Microtubule repolymerization and restoration of axonal transport.

---

## 3. Architecture

SHOA-Nerve consists of three components:

**Biocompatible Nerve Guide Conduit.** A collagen-chitosan tube embedded with CdSe/ZnS quantum dots arranged in M=10 clusters. The conduit bridges the gap between proximal and distal nerve stumps.

**Quantum Dot Sensor Array.** Each cluster monitors local Ψ-coherence via Spiked Voting. K=7 of 10 triggers the Grape-impulse.

**Handheld Grape-Emitter.** Delivers femtosecond Grape-impulses (120 fs, 0.35 eV) through the skin to the conduit.
